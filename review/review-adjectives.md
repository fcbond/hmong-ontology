# True adjectives: proposed ILIs, for Nathan White to check

White (2021: 144-146) lists eight true adjectives: a closed class of words that come
before the noun (Table 12), with *txawv* as a marginal ninth (fn. 48). Every other
word that WOLD calls an adjective is now written as a stative verb (2026-10-25).
These eight have no adjective senses in the lexicon yet. This sheet proposes
concepts for them.

**How to answer:** tick the concepts that fit (`[x]`), and add notes. A word can
have more than one concept; each ticked one becomes a sense. If none fits, say so,
and we will propose a new concept for the Interlingual Index. All concepts are from
Open English WordNet 2025. "Satellite" means an adjective that English WordNet
groups under a head adjective.

Section 1 covers the adjectives, Section 2 the stative verbs that four of them
share a form with (Table 13), and Section 3 asks again about *npawg*.

For each word, Heimbach (1979) and Mottin (1978) show what the two older
descriptions say. Page numbers are printed pages.

## 1. The adjectives

### 1.1 *me* (WH; also *mi*) / *miv* (GM) 'little, endearing'

- **White:** type II, from the stative verb *me* 'be small'. Adds the speaker's
  emotion: *kuv me phau ntawv* 'my (dear) little book' (p. 146, ex. 146).
- **Heimbach (p. 125):** *me* "Little, diminutive, small, small amount (size or
  quantity)". *me yaus* "This humble person (a polite term sometimes used in
  referring to oneself)" (p. 126).
- **Mottin (p. 48):** *me* "Petits (pour les êtres seuls)". *Me tub, me ntxhais,
  me noog* 'garçonnet, fillette, petit oiseau'; *Tus me npua me me no* 'ce tout
  petit cochon'.

| ✓ | ILI | Lemmas | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ ] | **i4698** | little | small in a way that arouses feelings (of tenderness or its opposite depending on the context) | *a nice little job* |
| [ ] | i9030 | little, small | (of children and animals) young, immature | *what a big little boy you are* |
| [ ] | i7988 | adorable, endearing, lovely | lovable especially in a childlike or naive way | |

Our first choice is in bold. i7579 'small' (limited or below average in size) is
the stative verb's concept (§2), so it is not offered here.

### 1.2 *me nyuam* (WH) / *miv nyuas* (GM) 'little'

- **White:** a hybrid: from a noun that was once adjective + noun, but often
  behaves as type II (p. 145).
- **Heimbach (p. 126):** only as the noun *menyuam* (tus) 'child, children'.
- **Mottin (p. 48):** *me nyuam* "Petits (pour les êtres aussi bien que les
  choses)". *Nyob ib lub me nyuam tsev* 'vivre dans une petite maison'; *Ua tej
  me nyuam hauj lwm* 'faire de petits travaux'.

| ✓ | ILI | Lemmas | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ ] | **i4698** | little | small in a way that arouses feelings (of tenderness or its opposite depending on the context) | *a nice little job* |
| [ ] | i7579 | small, little | limited or below average in number or quantity or magnitude or extent | *a little dining room* |

Mottin's examples (a small house, small jobs) read as plain size, which would be
i7579. If *me nyuam* also adds the speaker's view, i4698 fits.

### 1.3 *laus* (WH) / *laug* (GM) 'most important'

- **White:** type II, from the stative verb *laus* 'be old (animate)' (Table 13).
- **Heimbach (p. 107):** *laus* "Old, aged (of persons or animals), elder (contr.
  'qub')". *laus neeg*, *cov txwj laus* 'the village "elders", older persons whose
  counsel is respected'.
- **Mottin:** no adjective use. *tus laus neeg* 'l'ancien' (chef de village)
  (p. 28); *Pub niag laus noj* 'nourris tes vieux parents' (proverb, p. 205).

| ✓ | ILI | Lemmas | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ ] | **i6975** | chief, main, primary, principal, master | most important element | *the chief aim of living* |
| [ ] | i10181 | particular, special | first and most important | *his special interest is music* |
| [ ] | i11510 | senior | older; higher in rank; longer in length of tenure or service | |

Both older sources give 'elder' rather than 'most important'. i11510 'senior'
joins age and rank, in case that is the link.

### 1.4 *qub* 'old, former'

- **White:** type II, from the stative verb *qub* 'be old (inanimate)'. Old
  relative to the speaker's experience: *kuv lub qub tsev* 'my old house (i.e.,
  where I used to live)' (p. 155, ex. 164).
- **Heimbach (p. 265):** "Old (of inanimate objects) (contr. 'laus')". *lub qub
  zos* 'the old village'; *qub teb qub chaws* 'old or original place'. "Unlike most
  modifying words this word precedes rather than follows the word modified."
- **Mottin (p. 48):** separates the verb ("vieux, usagé") from the use before the
  noun ("ancien, précédent, celui d'avant"): *Lub zos qub lawm* 'le village est
  vieux', but *Kuv tus qub poj niam* 'mon ex-femme (décédée ou partie)' and *Cov
  qub khaub ncaws* 'les habits d'autrefois ou ceux que je portais hier'.

| ✓ | ILI | Lemmas | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ ] | **i9489** | erstwhile, former, old, onetime, quondam, sometime | belonging to some prior time | *erstwhile friend* |
| [ ] | i9490 | former, late, previous | (used especially of persons) of the immediate past | *the former president* |

### 1.5 *zoo* 'good, auspicious'

- **White:** type II, from the stative verb *zoo* 'be good, beautiful'. *lub niag
  zoo tsev* 'a good (auspicious) house' (p. 154, ex. 163).
- **Heimbach (p. 440):** *zoo* "Good (contr. 'phem')". *zoo neej* 'in good
  fortune, well-off'; *zoo nkauj* 'beautiful, admirable'.
- **Mottin (p. 49):** *zoo* "préféré comme adverbe": *Tau zoo niam thiab txiv*
  'être tombé sur de bons parents'; *Tus ntxhais tau zoo neej ua* 'cette fille
  réussit bien dans la vie'.

| ✓ | ILI | Lemmas | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ ] | **i951** | auspicious | auguring favorable circumstances and good luck | *an auspicious beginning for the campaign* |
| [ ] | i3358 | golden, favorable, lucky, prosperous | presaging or likely to bring good luck or a good outcome | *a favorable time to ask for a raise* |

i6163 'good' is the stative verb's concept (§2), so it is not offered here.

### 1.6 *tuam* 'big, important'

- **White:** type III, borrowed (from Chinese). Table 15 puts it under both
  dimension and value.
- **Heimbach (p. 329), tuam 7:** "The Hmong pronunciation of the Chinese word for
  'large' or 'great' or 'chief'". *tuam timxyoob* 'chief assistant'; *los tuam*
  'the eldest child'; *tuam tshawjchim* 'a great enemy'; *los nag tuam tsam* 'a
  heavy rain'.
- **Mottin (p. 48):** *tuam* "Grand, au physique comme au moral (du numéral
  chinois 1)". *Ib lub tuam tsev* 'une grande maison'; *Tus tuam vaj* 'un grand
  roi'.

| ✓ | ILI | Lemmas | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ ] | **i7530** | large, big | above average in size or number or quantity or magnitude or extent | *a large city* |
| [ ] | **i6968** | important, of import | of great significance or value | *important people* |
| [ ] | i6975 | chief, main, primary, principal | most important element | *the chief aim of living* |
| [ ] | i6981 | great, outstanding | of major significance or importance | |

We propose two senses: size (i7530) and importance (i6968).

### 1.7 *niag* (WH) / *nam* (GM) 'huge; important; mere; bad'

- **White:** type I, from the noun *niam* 'mother'. Historically 'big'
  (augmentative); 'bad' is a pejorative reading of 'big' (pp. 145, 155).
- **Heimbach (p. 140):** niag 1 "Large, great, major": *lub niag nroog* 'capital
  city, major city'; *lub niag tsev* 'the large house, palace, temple'. niag 2
  "Used in terms of familiarity": *niag yawg* 'familiar term of address for
  males'.
- **Mottin:** pejorative. *Niag ntawd!* 'ce type ! (méprisant)'; *Niag lwj!* 'ce
  bon à rien (litt. ce pourri)!' (p. 148, insults); *kuv niag niam* 'mon espèce de
  mère' (p. 8); *niag yawg* 'dis donc, vieux !' (p. 169, address).

| ✓ | ILI | Lemmas | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ ] | **i7552** | huge, immense, vast | unusually great in size or amount or degree or especially extent or scope | *huge government spending* |
| [ ] | **i6968** | important, of import | of great significance or value | *important people* |
| [ ] | **i6173** | bad | having undesirable or negative qualities | *a bad report card* |
| [ ] | i4954 | contemptible | deserving of contempt or scorn | |
| [ ] | i7530 | large, big | above average in size or number or quantity or magnitude or extent | *a large city* |
| [ ] | i6047 | mere | being nothing more than specified | *a mere child* |

Mottin's insults suggest the 'bad' reading expresses contempt (i4954) more than
poor quality (i6173). No concept here covers the familiar use in address
(*niag yawg*).

### 1.8 *nyuag* (WH) / *nyuas* (GM) 'small; important, special; bad'

- **White:** type I, from the noun *nyuam* 'child'. Historically 'small'
  (diminutive). *nyuas naag ncuav* 'small squirrels' (GM, p. 144, ex. 145).
- **Heimbach (p. 222):** *nyuag* is listed as the tone-changed form of *nyuam*:
  "Small, little, negligible (contr. 'niag')". *ib nyuag qhov* 'a little bit, a
  little place'; *ob peb nyuag hnub* 'a few days'.
- **Mottin (p. 48):** *nyuag* "Petits (un peu méprisant, ≠ tuam)". *Ib lub nyuag
  tsev* 'une maison minuscule'; *Ib nyuag pliag* 'un tout petit instant'; *Tsis
  muaj ib tee nyuag dej* 'il n'y a pas la moindre goutte d'eau'.

| ✓ | ILI | Lemmas | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ ] | **i7579** | small, little | limited or below average in number or quantity or magnitude or extent | *a little dining room* |
| [ ] | i6968 | important, of import | of great significance or value | *important people* |
| [ ] | i2748 | especial, exceptional, particular, special | surpassing what is common or usual or expected | *he paid especial attention to her* |
| [ ] | i6173 | bad | having undesirable or negative qualities | *a bad report card* |
| [ ] | i7582 | bantam, diminutive, lilliputian, midget, petite | very small | |
| [ ] | i7584 | dinky, insignificant | small and unimpressive | |

Mottin's 'minuscule' and 'un peu méprisant' suggest i7582 or i7584 rather than
plain i7579.

### 1.9 *txawv* 'different, foreign' (marginal)

- **White:** an adjective only with *teb chaws* 'country': 'other countries'
  (fn. 48). Elsewhere a stative verb, 'be different, be strange'.
- **Heimbach (p. 376):** "To differ, to deviate, different".
- **Mottin (p. 57):** the verb only: *Kuv lub tsev txawv koj lub* 'ma maison est
  différente de la tienne'.

Since it occurs with only one noun, we propose no adjective sense for *txawv*.
Is it better to add *txawv teb chaws* 'foreign countries' as a phrase?

## 3. *npawg*, again

On 17 October you said that the children of a father's **sister** are not
*npawg* or *muam npaws*, and that you were checking the literature. Heimbach's
kinship charts (Appendix 10) say they are, but only for a male speaker:

- **Chart 1, Paternal ancestry (p. 494):** the father's sister's son is *npawg*
  and her daughter *muam npaws*, "as called by △" (a male speaker). A female
  speaker calls them *yaum dab* and *vivncaus*.
- **Chart 2, Maternal ancestry (p. 495):** the same for the children of the
  mother's brother and of both her sisters: *npawg* and *muam npaws* for a male
  speaker, *yaum dab* and *vivncaus* for a female speaker.

By Heimbach, then, *npawg* is every first cousin outside the clan (the children
of the father's sister and of the mother's siblings), and it is used only by a man.
White (2021: 288) glosses it 'maternal male cousin', and we map it to KinDiv
Mo;Sb;So 'son of mother's sibling' as an exact match.

1. Do your speakers use *npawg* and *muam npaws* for the father's sister's children?
2. Are they used only by a male speaker? If so, are *yaum dab* and *vivncaus*
   what a woman says?

If Heimbach is right, the KinDiv mapping is not exact, and we would propose new
concepts for 'male speaker's cousin through the father's sister or the mother'.
